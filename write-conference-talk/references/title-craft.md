# Title craft — distilled from swyx's "Stop Writing Long Boring Titles"

Source: <https://dx.tips/titles> by Shawn "swyx" Wang.

## The thesis (one paragraph)

Bad titles prevent readers from accessing good content. Titles deserve roughly **50% of a writer's effort, not 5%.** The goal is a human-readable, quotable title that pulls people in through **an opinion or a curiosity gap** — not corporate jargon. "Imagine telling your friend about your talk." That's the bar.

## What NOT to do

- **No corporate weasel words.** Banned list (extend as you spot more): *leveraging, towards, navigating, empowering, unleashing, unlocking, revolutionizing*.
- **Resist colons.** swyx: *"colons lack confidence."* They usually signal you're hedging between two titles you can't choose between. Pick one.
- **Don't bury the lede** through excessive curiosity gaps. The reader should know within 6 seconds what the talk is about.
- **No self-centered formulas** like *"with X and Y open source"* — the speaker's tool stack is not the headline.
- **Anything over 3 syllables is a red flag.** Simple Anglo-Saxon words beat Latinate ones.
- **SEO keywords belong in the abstract, not the title.** Titles are for humans.

## What TO do

- **Make the title quotable** — short enough to repeat, opinionated enough to remember.
- **Prescribe a strong opinion** rather than vague promises.
- **Use a question** if it sparks a real curiosity gap (not rhetorical questions like *"Are you ready for AI agents?"*).
- **Pick a buzzword on the rise**, not at saturation. Test regularly and transition before everyone else catches up.

## Six title templates (madlibs)

These are swyx's named patterns. Use them as scaffolding when generating candidates.

| # | Template | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **{BENEFIT} without {DOWNSIDE}** | *"How to Get Rich (Without Getting Lucky)"*; *"How to Market Yourself (Without Being A Celebrity)"* |
| 2 | **{OPINION}, don't {THING YOU DO}** | *"Parse, don't validate"* |
| 3 | **{POPULAR THING} IS DEAD** | *"Prompt Engineering is Dead"*; *"Big Data is Dead"*. Lighter variant: *"You Might Not Need {THING}"* |
| 4 | **The Rise of {THING}** / **The Unreasonable Effectiveness of {THING}** | *"Rise of AI Engineer"*; *"The Unreasonable Effectiveness of LLMs in Code"* |
| 5 | **Fundamentals of {THING}** / **How {THING} Really Works** / **Advanced {THING}** | Authority play — works when you actually own the topic |
| 6 | **{EXPERIENCE/SCALE} with {THING}** | *"What We Learned from a Year of Building with LLMs"* |

## Good examples (with why)

| Title | Why it works |
|---|---|
| *Simple Made Easy* | Strong opinion, 3 words, naming a mental model (Rich Hickey) |
| *Inventing on Principle* | Quotable, prescriptive — Bret Victor |
| *What Clayton Christensen Got Wrong* | Names a target, picks a fight |
| *Rise of AI Engineer* | Template-4, claimed a new role-category that took off |
| *Does AI Actually Boost Developer Productivity? (100k Devs Study)* | Real curiosity gap + numbers proving stakes |
| *Real Agent Evals: Evals that don't suck* | Prescriptive opinion + named target (other people's evals) |

## Bad examples (with why)

| Title | What's broken |
|---|---|
| *Leveraging GenAI with Robust Data Foundations to Transform Businesses* | Every weasel word at once. No opinion, no specificity. |
| *How to Build AI Agents that Actually Work* | "Actually work" is the only piece of opinion and it's a cliché. |
| *Building enterprise LLM agents that work* | Vague promise, no stakes, no opinion. |
| *Lessons from building GenAI based applications* | "GenAI based applications" is two abstractions away from anything concrete. |
| *Working with Coding Agents* | Verb-noun-noun. Reads like a YouTube tutorial folder name. |

swyx's note: *"These succeed despite their titles, not because of them."*

## Key frameworks

### The Three Strikes Rule

Once you've repeated yourself three times — even if you're quoting others — ship it as your own. Credit the source, but **make the opinion yours**. The opinion gets stronger each time you say it; by the third repetition you've earned the right to put it in a title.

### Mental models and frameworks

Complex opinions need a scaffold the audience can carry away. Naming the scaffold is half the work:

- *Radiating Circles*
- *Third Age of JavaScript*
- *The Rise of the AI Engineer*

When you've got a mental model worth keeping, **name it in the title** — the model becomes the takeaway.

### Title-first writing

Write the title first, then aggressively reshape content to serve the promise. Titling after the talk is finished is how you end up with weasel-word titles — you're trying to summarize, not provoke.

## The closing rule

> You should break the rules once you understand human behavior more intuitively than rules can model.

Translation: learn the templates, then earn the right to break them.

## Quick checklist for a candidate title

- [ ] Could you say this out loud to a friend without flinching?
- [ ] Does it state an opinion or open a real curiosity gap?
- [ ] No colons (or if there's one, it earns its place — usually it doesn't)
- [ ] Under 8 words, ideally 5-6
- [ ] No banned weasel words
- [ ] Words ≤3 syllables where possible
- [ ] Names a concrete target, mental model, claim, or contrast
- [ ] Reads more like a quote than a brochure
